Project Overview

As an undergraduate research assistant with the Ryan Lab at Duke University, I spent two weeks conducting fieldwork on the Greenland ice sheet near Kangerlussuaq. Our goal was to quantify meltwater runoff by measuring ice sheet surface height change, ice density, and stream and lake water velocity at four field sites within the ice sheet, spanning a mix of supraglacial lakes and streams.

For the duration of the deployment, our team lived in a tent camp on the tundra just outside the ice sheet margin, hiking out to our field sites daily to collect measurements under variable weather and terrain conditions. The work demanded constant adaptation to the logistics of remote glacial fieldwork, from equipment care to route planning across shifting ice and meltwater features.

Part of the trip also involved field-testing a prototype robotic monitoring system designed to eventually support this kind of data collection. Observing the operational issues that came up during on-ice testing gave our team a clearer picture of what the system still needs to handle real field conditions. I haven't yet worked hands-on with the drone hardware itself, my role so far has been focused on field data collection, but I'm now joining the drone redesign team to help troubleshoot and address the issues we identified during testing.
